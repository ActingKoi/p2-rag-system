# 1000 Ways to Die

1000 Ways to Die is an American docufiction anthology television series that originally aired on Spike and later on Comedy Central from May 14, 2008, to July 15, 2012. The series dramatizes unusual deaths, some of which are based on true events or debunked urban legends. It features interviews with experts who discuss the scientific principles behind each case. Until the end of the first season, episodes concluded with real-life footage of near-fatal incidents and interviews with those involved.
Ron Perlman served as narrator beginning with the third episode, following Thom Beers's narration of the first two. "Shotgun Tom" Kelly later replaced Perlman during the final season.
A spin-off special, 1000 Ways to Lie, aired once in 2010, and a graphic novel adaptation was later released. The show received generally mixed to negative reviews during its run.

Format and style
1000 Ways to Die uses a darkly comedic approach to depict deaths drawn from myths and historical incidents through dramatized reenactments of the fatal incidents, accompanied by witness testimonials, scientific explanations and computer-generated imagery (CGI). Names, locations, and other details were often altered for narrative effect. Despite its title, the series depicted only 497 deaths, with some international versions increasing the total to 517.
While some segments were based on actual events such as the deaths of Harry Houdini, Jack Daniel, Mary Mallon, and Sigurd Eysteinsson, others incorporated fictionalized or exaggerated elements for dramatic purposes. A recurring theme involves individuals whose reckless, ill-considered or immoral actions lead to their deaths, often portrayed as ironic consequences of their own behavior. Segment titles commonly feature puns or wordplay related to the manner of death. Some deaths depicted in the series have been nominated for or received a Darwin Award.

Opening sequence
Each episode of 1000 Ways to Die opens with a disclaimer warning viewers not to recreate or imitate the incidents shown. Several variations of this warning were used throughout the program's run and across different broadcast regions.
In the original United States airing, the disclaimer stated that the stories were based on real deaths and were "extremely graphic," ending with the phrase: "Do not attempt to try any of the actions depicted... you will die!" It also included a disclaimer stating that the names of the deceased had been changed to protect their identities.
International versions featured localized adaptations. The United Kingdom broadcast added the line, "Some of the dead were unlucky; many were bored; most were incredibly stupid," while the Australian version retained the American wording with minor alterations.
Following the warning, a narration introduces the series' premise, which presents different types and circumstances of death including germs, toxins, injury, illness, and catastrophe.

Production
The series was created by Thom Beers and produced by Original Productions, a company founded by Beers. The two pilot episodes had a different style from the main series and were narrated by Beers for the American broadcast.
For the American broadcast, Ron Perlman narrated the series before being replaced by "Shotgun Tom" Kelly for the final episodes.
For the British broadcast, Alisdair Simpson narrated the pilot and series 1–2, followed by Howard Ritchie for series 3–4. John Moore narrated the pilot and series 1–4 for the Australian broadcast.

Broadcast history
Spike ordered additional episodes of the series in April 2009, including a pilot for the proposed spin-off 1000 Ways to Lie. In January 2010, the network ordered a third season. In October 2011, Spike ordered a fourth season consisting of 13 episodes, which was scheduled to premiere in March 2012.
Production was halted in February 2012 following a labor dispute involving the production company and crew members. Spike confirmed that production on the fourth season had concluded.
